## Q1. Clause-type marking

**Finding:** There is a clear clause-initial marker for one clause type. The form "máax" occurs 4 times in the corpus (per tokens.csv) and in all 4 cases it is the first word of its clause, and in all 4 cases that clause is closed with a question mark. No other clause in the corpus opens with "máax," and no clause closed with "?" fails to open with "máax." This is a clean form-to-punctuation correlation, so I treat "máax" as a recurring clause-initial marker for one clause type (call it type Q), position initial.

Two weaker candidates for other clause-initial marking exist but do not reach the same strength. "otsuks" opens 3 lines (38, 42, 48), always as the very first word, each time followed by a full clause; this looks like a recurring initial slot-filler but the clauses that follow it are of mixed shape (one is itself type Q), so it may mark utterance onset rather than a clause type proper. "'ayok" opens 2 lines (27, 31), and both of those lines are closed with a period rather than "!" or "?"; other period-closed lines (32, 33, 36) do not open with "'ayok," so the correlation only runs one direction and is thin.

**Evidence (line numbers):** máax-initial, question-closed: 40, 42, 44, 46. otsuks-initial: 38, 42, 48. 'ayok-initial, period-closed: 27, 31.

**Confidence:** High for the máax pattern (type Q, initial position). Low for otsuks and 'ayok as clause-type markers (both plausible discourse-initial forms, but not confirmed as type markers).

## Q2. Noun-phrase order

**Finding:** One recurring form, "le," gives a clean distributional result. "le" occurs 4 times total, and in every occurrence it immediately precedes a noun-like form (a form that also occurs bare elsewhere as a one-word exclamation): "le Vaal" (line 10), "le mucane" (line 27), and "le le Vaal" with two adjacent instances of "le" before the same noun-like form (line 35). Since "le" never follows a noun-like form and always precedes one, I treat it as an article-like or determiner-like form with fixed prenominal position.

I could not identify a distinct possessive-like or modifier-like class from distribution alone that contrasts with "le" in a minimal pair. The only candidate is "u" in line 27 ("u mujuk' le mucane"), which precedes a different form than "le" does in the same line, but it occurs only once in the whole corpus, so I cannot confirm it belongs to a separate class or fix its position relative to a noun-like form beyond this single case.

**Evidence (line numbers):** le + noun-like form: 10, 27, 35 (twice in 35). Candidate second pre-nominal form ("u"), single attestation: 27.

**Confidence:** Medium for article-like "le" being fixed prenominal (small but 100% consistent sample, n=4). Undetermined, effectively low, for any separate possessive-like or modifier-like ordering.

## Q3. Possession order

**Finding:** Undetermined. The only candidate for a possessor-possessed relation is the single-occurrence sequence "u mujuk'" in line 27, where "u" precedes "mujuk'." With only one attestation, and no second case pairing "u" (or any other candidate possessor-like form) with a different possessed-like form, I cannot establish a repeatable order. No minimal pair (same two forms, or same class of forms, in reversed order) is present anywhere else in the corpus.

**Evidence (line numbers):** 27 (single candidate instance only).

**Confidence:** Low (undetermined; evidence is a single token pair).

## Q4. Verb-object order

**Finding:** Two recurring forms, "a'te" (11 occurrences) and "u'te" (6 occurrences), show the same distributional behavior: each is immediately followed by exactly one other form (never preceded within the same clause by a form that itself follows a'te/u'te), and that following form varies from line to line ('Ibil, ik'el, líimek, yuquia, waja, fukuur, itsok, itsok, Kuxkal for a'te; kuxkal, cha'tsoke, puyao, ik'el, mucane, mucane for u'te). This head-plus-following-form pattern is stable whether a'te/u'te opens the clause or follows an interjection like "Otsuks" or "Xi." A third, rarer form with the same shape, "tzokan'te" (2 occurrences, lines 18 and 38), behaves the same way, always followed by a single form ("ik'el," "u'te ik'el").

If a'te/u'te/tzokan'te are treated as a verb-like class (recurring clause head, obligatorily followed by one complement slot) and the forms that follow them as an object-like class, the distributional order is head-first: verb-like form precedes object-like form in every attestation (17 tokens across the three related forms, 0 counterexamples).

**Evidence (line numbers):** a'te + following form: 6 (twice), 15, 16, 19, 24, 28, 29, 35, 37, 40. u'te + following form: 23, 35, 37, 38, 43, 45. tzokan'te + following form: 18, 38.

**Confidence:** Medium. The head-before-complement order is very consistent, but I cannot independently confirm that a'te/u'te are "verbs" and their following forms are "objects" rather than, say, aspect/mood particles plus predicate; the positional claim (head precedes complement) is the safer part of the finding.

## Q5. Copula

**Finding:** Undetermined. I looked for a recurring linking form sitting between two noun-like forms in an equational reading, and did not find one. The clearest juxtaposed pairs in the corpus are of the head-plus-following-form type already described in Q4 (e.g., "A'te 'Ibil!" line 15, "A'te ik'el!" line 16), which look more like a predicate-plus-complement structure than a bare noun-equals-noun equational clause, so they do not resolve the copula question either way.

One recurring medial form, "ka" (3 occurrences: lines 40, 42, 44), sits in a fixed slot inside the question frame "Máax ka ti ___?" This could in principle be some kind of linking or auxiliary element, but it is confined to that one frame and never appears outside it, so I cannot generalize it to a copula function in ordinary equational or juxtaposed clauses.

**Evidence (line numbers):** Juxtaposed head+complement pairs without an overt linker: 15, 16. Fixed-frame medial form "ka": 40, 42, 44.

**Confidence:** Low (undetermined either way; no clear equational-clause minimal pair to test for an overt copula).

## Q6. Affix position

**Finding:** The distribution supports bound elements attaching on both sides (front and back), not just one.

Front-attached recurring strings across otherwise distinct forms: "ik'-" in ik'bala (line 4), ik'el (lines 6 twice, 11, 16, 18, 38), and ik'eche (line 32); "ko'-" in ko'janti (line 14), ko'soxsal (line 33), and ko'mujuk (line 34); "a'-" in a'te (11 occurrences, see Q4) and a'tul (lines 40, 42); "u'-" in u'te (6 occurrences, see Q4) and u'tra (line 28).

Back-attached recurring strings across otherwise distinct forms: "-'te" in a'te, u'te, xu'te (line 8), and tzokan'te (lines 18, 38); "-tul" in a'tul (lines 40, 42), tul (line 8), and jare'yantul (line 50).

Independent attestation of a bare stem is rare (the clearest case is "u" occurring alone in line 27, alongside "u'te" and "u'tra" elsewhere), so segmentation of these shared strings into true morpheme boundaries is inferential rather than confirmed. Still, the same short strings recur at the same edge (front or back) across multiple otherwise-unrelated forms often enough that I do not think it is coincidental.

**Evidence (line numbers):** Front strings: ik'- at 4, 6, 11, 16, 18, 32, 38; ko'- at 14, 33, 34; a'- and u'- throughout the lines listed in Q4 plus 28, 40, 42. Back strings: -'te at 6, 8, 15, 16, 18, 19, 23, 24, 28, 29, 35 (x2), 37, 38, 40, 43, 45; -tul at 8, 40, 42, 50.

**Confidence:** Medium (pattern is recurrent and two-sided, but morpheme-boundary segmentation is not independently confirmed by enough bare-stem attestations).

## Closing note

The strongest results in this sheet are the clause-initial question marker "máax" (Q1) and the head-before-complement order shown by a'te/u'te/tzokan'te (Q4), both resting on clean, repeated, exceptionless distributions. The article-like prenominal "le" (Q2) and the two-sided affix pattern (Q6) are reasonably supported but rest on smaller or less directly confirmable samples. Possession order (Q3) and the presence or absence of a copula (Q5) could not be resolved from this corpus; both would need more minimal pairs than are attested here.
