## Q1. Clause-type marking

**Finding:** Clause type appears to be marked, but by several recurring devices in mixed positions rather than one uniform marker. Exclamatory or imperative-like clauses correlate with clause-final `!` and often with a high-frequency initial form (`yutsal`, `mucane`, `ik'el`, `kutsen`, `tlayeb`, and similar). Interrogative-like clauses correlate with `?` (on a medial form or on the whole utterance) and with the recurring pair `ka` / `ti` in equational-looking strings. A recurring clause-initial pair `en` (sometimes followed by `ta'`) opens at least one multi-clause line. Vocative-like address uses a name followed by comma before another form. Punctuation (`!`, `?`, `,`, `.`, `...`) co-occurs with these patterns but is not fully consistent.

**Evidence:** Exclamatory/imperative-like: lines 2, 4, 6, 11, 15-19, 21, 43, 45. Interrogative-like with `?` and `ka`/`ti`: lines 40, 42, 44, 46, 49. Clause-initial `en` / `en` + `ta'`: lines 27, 49. Vocative comma after name: lines 1, 3, 5, 7, 31, 48.

**Confidence:** medium

## Q2. Noun-phrase order

**Finding:** Tentative determiner-like forms (`le`, `te`, `ek`, `ta'`, `u`) tend to precede a following noun-like form when they co-occur in the same phrase: `le` before `Vaal` (10); `te` before `Atziri` (1, 7); `ek` before `te` (1) and before `Ti` (50); `ta'` before `'Ayok` (27); `u` before `niáach` (27). Modifier-like forms also precede a following noun-like form in some strings: `ascensionada` / `ascenada` before `Atziri` (2, 7); `kujkuali` before `itsok` (4, 13). A bound-looking element `ko'...` follows a preceding form in line 14 (`Gyan'uks ko'janti`). Article-like versus modifier-like classes are not fully separable from the data; full NP template remains incomplete.

**Evidence:** Determiner-like before noun-like: lines 1, 7, 10, 27, 50. Modifier-like before noun-like: lines 2, 4, 7, 13. Postposed bound modifier: line 14.

**Confidence:** low

## Q3. Possession order

**Finding:** Only one phrase offers a possible possession string: `u niáach mucane` (27), where `u` precedes `niáach`, which precedes `mucane`. If `niáach` is possessor-like and `mucane` is head-like, possessor material appears before the possessed form. Line 37 (`u'te pu uch' puyao`) is structurally opaque and does not confirm order. Overall possession order is not securely identifiable.

**Evidence:** line 27 (possible possessor-before-possessed); line 37 (ambiguous, non-confirming).

**Confidence:** low

## Q4. Verb-object order

**Finding:** A verb-like class is not established with confidence. Recurring predicate-like forms (`kifba`, `kilya`, `kutsen`, `tlayeb`, `ik'el`) often appear clause-initially or immediately before `A'te` / `a'te`, but the same forms also cluster with each other (`tlayeb kifba`, 22; `kifba kilya`, 31) without a clear object slot. Noun-like forms (`Atziri`, `Kextal`, `itsok`) sometimes sit between other forms and sometimes follow modifier-like material, but no stable verb-to-object adjacency rule emerges. Relative verb-object order is undetermined.

**Evidence:** Predicate-like clustering without clear object: lines 6, 22, 31, 40. Noun-like forms in longer chains without fixed verb-object pairing: lines 4, 13, 35, 37.

**Confidence:** low (finding: undetermined)

## Q5. Copula

**Finding:** Mixed. Some equational or identifying-looking clauses use an overt linker frame built from `ka` and `ti`: `ti ka` before a name-like form (40, 42) and `ka ti` after a name-like form (44). Many other clauses rely on juxtaposition with comma or period only (27, 14, 31). No single copula form appears in all equational strings.

**Evidence:** Overt `ka` / `ti` frame: lines 40, 42, 44. Juxtaposition without `ka`/`ti`: lines 14, 27, 31.

**Confidence:** medium

## Q6. Affix position

**Finding:** Front-bound elements are attested as prefixes or proclitics on several recurring shapes: `ko'-` (`ko'janti`, 14; `ko'soxsal`, 33; `ko'mujuk`, 34), `ik'-` (`ik'el`, 6, 11, 16, 18, 38; `ik'bala`, 4; `ik'eche`, 32; `ikba'yucane`, 5), and `u'-` (`u'te`, 37-39, 43, 45; `u'tra`, 28; `U'Te`, 23). Apostrophe-internal splits (`jare'yantul`, 50; `le'itzil`, 12; `cha'tsoke`, 35) show compound-like bonding but do not by themselves establish productive suffixation. Back-bound suffixes are not securely demonstrated.

**Evidence:** Front-bound `ko'-`: lines 14, 33, 34. Front-bound `ik'-`: lines 4-6, 11, 16, 18, 32, 38. Front-bound `u'-`: lines 23, 28, 35, 37-39, 43, 45. Internal bonding: lines 12, 35, 50.

**Confidence:** medium (front-bound); suffix position undetermined
