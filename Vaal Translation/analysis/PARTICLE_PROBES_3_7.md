## 1. *ka* and *ti* in *Máax ka ti a'tul*

### Every attestation

| Line | Surface | Following material | Question frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | *Máax ka ti a'tul?* | *a'tul* (soft: *a-* + *taal* "come," §10.9) | *Máax ka ti* + verb-like |
| 42 | *Máax ka ti a'tul?* | same | same |
| 44 | *Máax ka ti cheyel?* | *cheyel* (soft: Nah. *chiya* + Maya *-el*, §10.9) | *Máax ka ti* + verb-like |
| 46 | *Máax tlayeb mucane?* | *tlayeb mucane* (prenominal modifier + noun) | *Máax* + noun phrase, **no** *ka ti* |

That is the closed set. *ka* and *ti* do not appear together anywhere else. Isolated *ti / Ti / ti'* as a dative/locative is a different token (§9 *Ti*, *te*). Isolated *ka* does not recur.

### Competing readings

| ID | Reading | What predicts it | Palette lead (not a premise) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Subordinator / dependent *ka* plus relational *ti'* | *ka ti* only before verb-like words; dropped before a noun phrase | Yucatec *ka(j)* "that / when"; *ti'* "to / at" (already a §3.8 lead) |
| B | Interrogative reinforcement (both particles mark the question) | would appear in every *Máax* line | none required |
| C | Aspect or mood on the following verb | *a'tul* / *cheyel* would then be inflected stems | Yucatec aspect particles sit near the verb |
| D | Overt copula / linker | scrambled-panel analyst D treated *ka* / *ti* as a linker | Spanish *que* / copula analogy, forbidden as a source |
| E | Opaque: two unanalysable syllables | honest miss | none |

### What would falsify each

- **A** fails if *ka ti* appears between *Máax* and a bare noun predicate, or if *a'tul* and *cheyel* are shown to be nouns. It also fails if a new line has *Máax* + verb without *ka ti* in the same register.
- **B** fails because line 46 is already a *Máax* question without *ka ti*. Reinforcement that is optional only before verbs collapses into A.
- **C** fails if the same verb-like appears without *ka ti* in a non-question, or if *ka ti* appears with a noun. Not testable on this corpus (*a'tul* and *cheyel* are hapax in that slot).
- **D** fails if equational clauses elsewhere remain juxtaposed with no *ka/ti* (they do: *Atziri le'itzil*, Text 1 frame). Using *ka ti* as a general copula overpredicts. Gloss-blind panel: D (scrambled) imposed this reading; real-panel A/B did not. That is a negative control, not a vote for D.
- **E** fails only if a distributional regularity appears. Line 46 vs 40/42/44 is already such a regularity, so E is weaker than A.

### Verdict: **leaning** (A)

Preferred: *ka ti* is a verbal-question linker (subordinator plus relational, or an unanalysed pair with that distribution). Residual doubt: *a'tul* and *cheyel* are themselves soft, so "verb-like" is a distributional label, not a committed lexical class. Do not import Yucatec *ka(j)* as the Vaal rule. Do not treat *ka ti* as a copula. Master §3.7 updated to this lean; §3.8 lead unchanged.

Possession is not involved. No claim here about *u mujuk' le mucane*.

## 2. *le le Vaal* doubling

### Every attestation

| Line | Surface | Context |
|---|---|---|
| 35 | *... xefe yotlapek le le Vaal te'moxti qexcan...* | article(s) before the name *Vaal*, inside a long Text 3 clause |

*le* / *le'* also occurs singly: *le mucane* (line 27), *le'itzil* (line 12), *Tlaxye' le Vaal* (line 10). Doubling is hapax.

### Competing readings

| ID | Reading | What predicts it |
|---|---|---|
| A | Reduplicated article (emphasis, "the very Vaal") | only this name, or emphatic contexts |
| B | Two stacked determiners (article + article, or article + a second *le* lemma) | *le* would have two functions in the corpus |
| C | Scribal / oratorical repetition (same word said twice) | no second function; performance |
| D | Failed discontinuous article (*le ... o'* with the closer realized as a second *le*) | a closer would appear after the noun; here *Vaal* is followed by *te'moxti*, not *o'* or *le* |
| E | Second *le* is not the article (Cordemex *LE* "lazo; oh!") | the second token would be a different lemma |

Cordemex *LE* is both the demonstrative/article ("ese; el; aquel") and "lazo," and an exclamation. Exact-hit salad is the same problem as the translation battery. Distribution, not the dictionary, has to decide.

### What would falsify each

- **A** fails if doubled *le le* appears on ordinary nouns with no emphatic frame, or never appears again on names.
- **B** fails if no other stacked determiner exists (none does) and if *le* has only one slot (prenominal article, §3.4).
- **C** is unfalsifiable on a hapax except by a second independent capture of the same line without the doubling.
- **D** fails already: the Yucatec closer is *o'*, not *le*, and it follows the noun. The second *le* here precedes *Vaal*. Keep as a lead, not a reading (§3.8).
- **E** fails if the second *le* cannot be shown to have a different neighbor (it cannot: both sit in the article slot).

### Verdict: **still open**

N = 1. Prenominal article *le* is settled. The doubling is not. Do not pick A, C, or D in §3.7. Palette *le ... o'* remains a lead in §3.8, explicitly not a resolution.

## 3. *-yan-* in *jare'yantul*

### Every attestation

| Line | Surface | Immediate context |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | *Ti ek tala jare'yantul!* | Text 5, Kamasan Smith; *tala* "come" (C) precedes the string |

No other *yan* as a free word. *Yatle* is a different presentative, not evidence for a medial *-yan-*.

### Competing readings

| ID | Segmentation | Medial *-yan-* | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | *jare'* + *yan* + *tul* | Yucatec *yan* "to exist / there is / have" (Cordemex *YAN* "ser o estar en el mundo; haber o tener") | *jare'* is the K'iche' connective (§10.2); *tul* "wane" is C\* |
| B | *jare'* + *yantul* | unanalysed tail | avoids committing *yan* |
| C | *jareyan* + *tul* | no medial *yan* | *jareyan* is not an attested stem |
| D | unsegmented opaque | none | whole-form lookup miss |

### What would falsify each

- **A** fails if *yan* never appears as a free existential, and if *tul* in this string cannot bear "wane" (the English "your waning" is gold, not evidence). A also needs *jare'* to be a separate word; the surface is one token.
- **B** and **C** fail if a second *yan* surfaces in the same slot, or if a space/audio break is documented inside the string (none is).
- **D** is the default under anti-forcing for the whole string. It does not erase the committed pieces *jare* and *tul* already in §9 / §10.2.

### Verdict: **still open**

The segmentation is proposed, not shown. *yan* as existential is the best dictionary piece for the medial, and it would fit "you come, and so there is waning." That is a gloss-shaped story. Form-first: the string is a hapax compound. Master §3.7 keeps the role of *-yan-* open. Do not add *yan* as a §9 row (it still has none).

## 4. *ta'* in *'Ayok ta' en*

### Every attestation

| Line | Surface | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | *'Ayok ta' en,* | privative + *ta'* + *en* |
| 49 | *Kíibsa' ta' en!* | imperative (soft "kill") + *ta'* + *en* |
| 37 | *ta'nuk* | possibly *ta'* + *nuk* "great one" (the *ta'-* of the §9 row) |

Always next to a person index: *-en* "me" (C\*), or the addressee *nuk*. Never clause-initial. Never a presentative.

### Competing readings

| ID | Root | Gloss | Form | Frame fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Maya *ti'* | to / for / at | vowel *a* vs *i*; glottal kept | "no more **for** me"; "kill **before** me" |
| B | Maya *taak* | to want | needs *k* → glottal | "I want no more" (line 27); line 49 "I want kill" is worse |
| C | Maya *ta'* | excrement | **exact** Cordemex *TA'* "caca" etc. | fails both frames; already excluded in §10.7 as contextually impossible |
| D | K'iche' *ta'* | to ask (for a bride) | exact Christenson | fails the privative/imperative frames |
| E | opaque particle | none | none | honest if A/B both fail |

Battery T-M often took C (excrement) because it is the exact hit. That is a translation-battery false friend, same class as *ik'el* insect.

### What would falsify each

- **A** fails if *ta'* appears without a person index, or if a line needs "want" and cannot be locative. The *a* vs *i* leftover is the residual doubt.
- **B** fails on line 49 unless "kill, I want" is accepted, and it needs an unattested *k* drop. Weaker than A on form and on line 49.
- **C** is already falsified by the privative + pronoun frame and by *ta'nuk* as an address. Logged as a loser, not deleted.
- **D** fails the neighbor field (no marriage context).
- **E** fails if A (or B) can be stated as a distributional rule: *ta'* sits between a clause-type operator and a person index. That rule is visible.

### Verdict: **leaning** (A)

Preferred: relational "to / for / before" with *-en* "me." Residual: the vowel *ta'* vs *ti'* is not strict-safe, and *ta'nuk*'s *ta'-* may not be the same morpheme. Excrement stays in §10 as the exact-hit loser. Master §10.7 / §3.7 updated to this lean, not to a closed etymology.

## 5. *A'te / U'Te / Yatle* presentative alternation

### Every attestation (closed 51-line corpus)

**A'te / a'te** (clause-initial presentative + noun):

- 6 *A'te 'Ibil ... A'te ik'el ...*
- 15 *A'te 'Ibil!*
- 16 *A'te ik'el!*
- 19 *A'te líimek!*
- 24 *A'te yuquia!*
- 28 *A'te waja yatle u'tra buxa!* (A'te and Yatle in one line)
- 29 *A'te fukuur!*
- 35 *a'te itsok ...*
- 37 *a'te itsok ...*
- 40 *A'te Kuxkal tlayeb kutsen.*

**U'Te / u'te**:

- 23 *U'Te kuxkal!*
- 35 *Xi u'te cha'tsoke*
- 37 *u'te puyao*
- 38 *Tzokan'te u'te ik'el*
- 43 *U'te mucane!*
- 45 *U'te mucane!*

**Yatle**:

- 20 *Yatle yutsal!*
- 28 *A'te waja yatle u'tra buxa!*

Reduced *u'te* after a non-initial host is the same presentative, not a fourth lemma.

### Competing readings

| ID | Claim | What would support it |
|---|---|---|
| A | Pure phonological / register variation of one presentative | same slot, same following category (noun / NP); audio *U'Te* as /o/ |
| B | Deictic contrast (near *A'te* vs far *Yatle*, with *U'Te* medial or addressee-facing) | complementary distribution by referent distance |
| C | Person or stance contrast (*A-* 2sg vs *U-* 3sg vs *Ya-* locative) | *A'te* would prefer addressee-present objects; *U'Te* absent objects |
| D | Three unrelated function words | different following categories or different clause types |

### Distribution facts (no English)

- All three take a following nominal. None takes *Máax*. None is a privative.
- *Yatle* N = 2. One of those two co-occurs with *A'te* in the same clause (line 28): *A'te waja yatle u'tra buxa*. That is fatal to a simple complementary near/far split unless the line contains two presentations (here X, there Y). That reading is possible and is exactly the gold English, so it cannot be used as independent evidence. Distribution alone: two presentatives can co-occur.
- *A'te* vs *U'Te*: both present *ik'el* (lines 16 vs 38), both present *kuxkal* (40 vs 23), both present *mucane* only as *U'te* (43, 45). Not complementary.
- *U'Te* clusters in the drill (Text 4) and in the commander's long line. Register is possible. Not proven.

### What would falsify each

- **A** fails if a new contrastive pair shows meaning change with the same noun (*A'te X* vs *Yatle X* with opposite deixis, from audio or a stage direction).
- **B** fails if the same referent takes both forms, or if *Yatle* remains N = 2 with co-occurrence (already weak).
- **C** fails: *A'te ik'el* and *u'te ik'el* are the same type of object. 2sg vs 3sg does not track.
- **D** fails: following category is the same.

### Verdict: **leaning** (A), deictic **still open**

Preferred: one presentative with phonological and register variants (*A'te* default, *U'Te* /o/-onset variant, *Yatle* rarer). Residual: line 28 can be read as two deictic poles, and N for *Yatle* is 2. Master §3.7 keeps the deictic question open and records the lean that the slot is one presentative, not three copulas. Adversarial *ahtle* "nothing" stays in §10.12 (opposite sense, context favors presentative).

## 6. *tlayeb* attributive vs fronted locative

Etymology is in `RIVAL_READING_TOURNAMENTS.md` (keep C+L; ladder is a cross-graft loser). This worksheet is category.

### Every attestation

| Line | String | Immediately follows | Immediately precedes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | *tlayeb kutsen* | *'Ibil* | *kutsen* |
| 6 | *tlayeb kifba* | *ik'el* | *kifba* |
| 21 | *Tlayeb kutsen!* | (clause-initial) | *kutsen* |
| 22 | *Tlayeb kifba!* | (clause-initial) | *kifba* |
| 40 | *tlayeb kutsen* | *Kuxkal* | *kutsen* |
| 40 | *tlayeb ukto* | *Ela* | *ukto* |
| 46 | *tlayeb mucane* | *Máax* | *mucane* |

Seven tokens. Always immediately before a noun. Never after the noun. Never with *le* between *tlayeb* and the noun (*tlayeb le X* is unattested).

### Competing readings

| ID | Category | Paraphrase without using gold English | Order implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Attributive adjective / modifier | *tlayeb* is a dependent of the following noun | prenominal modifier, already §3.4 |
| B | Fronted locative / adverbial | *tlayeb* is a clause-level "in the dark" and the noun is the presented object | still before the noun; not a postnominal counterexample |
| C | Noun ("the dark") in construct with a following noun | two-noun compound or juxtaposition | still head-final dependents if the first is the modifier |

Gloss-blind panel: modifier category was **undetermined** for A and B; C left modifier undetermined. Historical SYN-1 (not gloss-blind) raised B. That is not independent confirmation.

### What would falsify each

- **A** fails if *tlayeb* appears without a following noun, or after the noun, or with an article between (*le tlayeb kutsen* vs *tlayeb le kutsen*). None of those exist.
- **B** fails if *tlayeb* is ever inside a clearly nested NP after an article (*le tlayeb kutsen* as "the dark offering" would actually support A). B is hard to falsify because fronted locatives also sit at the left of the noun.
- **C** fails if *tlayeb* inflects as an adjective (no adjective morphology is visible on anything in this corpus).

### Verdict: **still open** (category); prenominal position **settled**

§3.4 already states the order rule. This probe does not upgrade category to settled. Gold English often translates B ("in the dark, the offering"); that is a translation choice, not a distributional proof. Do not overclaim. Master §3.7 keeps the adjective-versus-adverbial status open.
