## Q1

**Finding:** Clause type is marked at least for one punctuation-defined question class. `Máax` recurs clause-initially in four questions. In three of them it is followed by `ka`; one lacks `ka`, so `ka` is not established as obligatory clause-type marking. `Axba` is another punctuation-defined question-like standalone form, but it occurs only once and cannot be classified further.

**Evidence:** `Máax` is initial in lines 42, 44, and 46, and initial in the second clause of line 40. The sequence `Máax ka` occurs in lines 40, 42, and 44, while line 46 has `Máax tlayeb`. `Axba` occurs in line 49.

**Confidence:** high for initial `Máax`; low for the status of `ka` and `Axba`.

## Q2

**Finding:** A narrow article-like pattern can be identified: `le` precedes a noun-like form. `le` immediately precedes recurring `Vaal` and `mucane`, and is never attested after them. Line 35 contains `le le Vaal`, so the internal analysis of that expanded sequence is uncertain. A possessive-like form and a modifier-like form cannot be securely identified. `u` and `pu` are possible prenominal elements, but each occurs only once. `tlayeb` precedes several forms, but its independent clause distribution favors a predicate-like analysis over a modifier-like one.

**Evidence:** `le Vaal` in line 10, `le mucane` in line 27, and `le le Vaal` in line 35. Possible prenominal sequences are `u mujuk'` in line 27 and `pu uch'` in line 37. `tlayeb` precedes `kutsen`, `kifba`, `ukto`, and `mucane` in lines 6, 21, 22, 40, and 46.

**Confidence:** medium for article-like `le` before a noun-like form; low for possible possessive-like elements; modifier order undetermined.

## Q3

**Finding:** The order of possessed and possessor is undetermined. No recurrent pair can be independently identified as those two classes. The sequences containing `u`, `pu`, or doubled `le` are too sparse and structurally ambiguous.

**Evidence:** `u mujuk' le mucane` in line 27, `pu uch' ta'nuk` in line 37, and `le le Vaal` in line 35.

**Confidence:** low.

## Q4

**Finding:** A predicate-like form followed by an object-like form is supported. `tlayeb` recurs before several interchangeable second-position forms and can begin a two-form clause. The safest distributional statement is predicate-like before complement-like. Calling the second class object-like is plausible but not fully established. `kilya` also precedes two different forms, but the evidence is thinner.

**Evidence:** `tlayeb kutsen` in lines 6, 21, and 40; `tlayeb kifba` in lines 6 and 22; `tlayeb ukto` in line 40; and `tlayeb mucane` in line 46. `kilya Zerphi` occurs in line 3 and `kilya sakilja` in line 31.

**Confidence:** medium.

## Q5

**Finding:** No invariant overt copula appears in recurrent two-part clauses. The corpus therefore supports juxtaposition without an overt copula for at least one predicational pattern. Whether these clauses are strictly equational cannot be determined from form distribution alone.

**Evidence:** The parallel clauses `Teoyuxtlane ascensionada` and `Teoyuxtlane Yutsal` in line 2 have no intervening form. Other uninterrupted two-form clauses include lines 13, 14, 17, 18, 20, 21, and 22.

**Confidence:** medium for absence of an overt intervening copula in these patterns; low for an equational classification.

## Q6

**Evidence:** Front `ko'` in `ko'janti`, `ko'soxsal`, and `ko'mujuk` in lines 14, 33, and 34. Front `ik'` in `ik'bala`, `ik'el`, and `ik'eche` in lines 4, 6, 11, 16, 18, and 32. **Confidence:** medium.
