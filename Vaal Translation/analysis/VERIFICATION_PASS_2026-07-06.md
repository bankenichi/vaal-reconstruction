## 2. Discrepancies found (should be fixed)

Ordered by importance.

1. **The 13 bare-L rows in §9 sit outside the statistical universe.** The §9 table has 75 rows, not 62: the 62 tiered tokens plus 13 entries carrying only "L" (*Gyan'uks, kíimil, le, líimek, Ma'oxe, Tlaxye', Xatlene, yax, yuquia, chikula', ko'janti, Tzokan'te,* and one more). The project's own taxonomy (AGENTS.md 2b, §9 key, HARDENING_PROTOCOL) defines L as an orthogonal tag that combines with a base tier (H+L, C+L), never standalone, so a bare L is undefined. More substantively, these 13 never went through either gate and are excluded from every headline denominator ("62 committed tokens"), yet several are load-bearing in the Text 1 and 2 translations (*kíimil*, *le*, *yuquia*, *Gyan'uks*). Recommend either running the two gates on them and assigning base tiers, or stating explicitly in §9 and §17 why they are exempt and that the 62-token statistics do not cover them.
2. **Eztli Pilli's Gate 1 record is not reproducible from the archived CSVs.** The strict-decode batches (`strict_committed_results_batch*.csv`) score it "soft," but `token_classification.csv` records "pass" and the token carries H. The blind Battery D runs support the pass (Eztli confirmed C in 5 of 5 seeds with online Nahuatl), so the classification is probably right on the merits, but the override is undocumented. As the files stand, the batches yield 21 strict passes + pul = 22, not the published 23. Add a one-line note (in EXPERIMENT_LOG and/or the CSV) recording the re-adjudication and its basis.
3. **Per-root Gate 2 figure is internally inconsistent by one.** §17.7 and STATISTICAL_SUMMARY report Gate 2 survival 48/53 alongside a per-root tier split of 22 H / 6 C* / 25 C. Six C* roots imply 47 survivors, not 48. The mismatch comes from inconsistent aggregation when a competitor-flagged token merges into a family whose other member survives (te into the Ti family, mucane into muk'). Pick one rule (worst-member or any-member) and apply it to both the tier split and the gate rate; as published the two cannot both be true.
4. **Adversarial survival is quoted with three different denominators.** 51/58 "effective" (ADVERSARIAL_RESULTS), 52/59 "testable, counting pul" (master §17.4), and 55/62 (STATISTICAL_SUMMARY gate table, which silently counts the 3 untestable names as survivors). All arithmetic is internally correct and all land near 88%, but a reader comparing documents will trip. State the canonical fraction once and derive the others from it.
5. **Two null-model plants are not committed tokens.** The 30 "real committed tokens" planted in every worksheet include *Kuxkal* and *Otsuks*, neither of which is in the 62-token committed lexicon (Otsuks is an open §10 item). Both scored "none" in the strict batteries, so the effect is a small downward bias on TPR and no effect on FPR or the null-rejection test, but the protocol text ("30 real committed tokens") is not strictly accurate. Note it, or swap the plants and re-pool.
6. **Pooled CIs overstate the independent evidence behind TPR.** The same 30 plant tokens are reused across all 5 seeds (union = 30), so the pooled "150 real" trials are 5 repeated measurements of 30 items, and 74 of the 500 pooled pseudo strings are cross-seed duplicates (426 unique). The binomial CIs on TPR treat n=150 as independent; the effective token sample is 30. The protocol's caveat ("treat TPR as indicative, not precise") is the right instinct and should be tied explicitly to this reuse. FPR and the headline null-rejection are essentially unaffected.
7. **Small documentation drift.** AGENTS.md §4 checklist expects "~1200" lines (actual: 1,328); EXPERIMENT_LOG says "master §8 tiers" where §9 is meant; citations [16] and [23] are defined in §18 but never cited by bracket marker in the body.

## 3. Methodology assessment

### 3.1 What is sound

The statistical architecture is unusually rigorous for this genre and, in several places, for amateur historical linguistics generally: a pre-registered null model with a clean 2x2 design and fixed seeds; blind scoring separated from key-holding; an adversarial gate with cognates correctly excluded as non-competitors; a syntax confirmation with a scrambled-corpus negative control that correctly demotes the order-independent findings; per-token and per-root dual accounting that anticipates the non-independence objection; PPV reported as a range over an unmeasurable base rate rather than a point; and a §17.10 that names the dominant unknowns honestly. The conclusions claimed are the conclusions the tests support, and the document repeatedly declines to overclaim (soft tier as noise, sentence-level fragility, certification rate versus correctness). The REVIEW.md critiques were genuinely acted on rather than buried.

### 3.2 Structural caveats that remain (mostly acknowledged, worth keeping visible)

- **Scorer asymmetry on the binomial test.** The 23 strict passes were certified by project-side decoding of known-real tokens, while the 4.6% noise floor came from blind agents. A leniency bias toward one's own lexicon would inflate the comparison. Mitigating check run during this pass: on the 30 tokens the two pipelines share, blind strict agents certify C at about the same rate the project scorer does (4-6 tokens each; *ek*, *Maax*, *naach*, *Eztli* blind-confirmed 4-5/5), so no large bias is visible, and the rejection survives any plausible deduction (even 15/62 at 4.6% is p < 10^-7). The §17.6 claim that the plant subset is deliberately hard is borne out: the project's own strict scoring passes only ~17-20% of the plant subset versus 37% of the full lexicon.
- **Analyst independence.** The blind decoders and syntax analysts are LLM agents. Their errors and priors are correlated in ways three human analysts' would not be, so the (1/2)^3-style chance baselines for unanimity are optimistic. The scrambled-corpus control is the strongest answer to this and is the right control; the unanimity p-values should be read as illustrative, not literal.
- **Gate 1 certifies the form, not the gloss.** Example surfaced in this pass: the strict batch passed *akal* via Cordemex "to quarrel/scold (Campeche)," a different sense from the committed "pond, eternal waters." The project already separates "genuine root" from "correct gloss" (§17.10), but readers of the H tier should be reminded that H hardens the derivation, not the translation choice.
- **English barks in the syntax corpus.** The analysts saw surface + English pairs, so some word-order conclusions (verb-object especially) could be partially recoverable from gloss alignment. Acknowledged in §17.10 as mild circularity; the scrambled control does not fully remove it because scrambling breaks the alignment too.

None of these overturn the two headline findings, which this pass independently confirms: (1) the committed lexicon's strict-pass rate is far beyond the measured noise floor, and (2) most of the committed lexicon is latitude-dependent and statistically indistinguishable from that floor, with the honest unit of confidence being the token.
